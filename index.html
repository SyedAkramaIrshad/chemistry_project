What the engine covers

This is a broad introductory/general-chemistry engine, not a quantum-chemistry simulator or universal synthesis predictor.

Structure rules

Common valence states, formal charges, ionic interactions, radicals, octet/duet checks, connected components, graph identity, formula, mass, and unsaturation.

Reaction families

Combustion, neutralization, acid–carbonate, activity-series displacement, halogen displacement, precipitation, combination, oxidation, and common decompositions.

Equation solver

Arbitrary formulas with parentheses, hydrates, and explicit ionic charge can be balanced when a positive unique solution exists.

Deliberate boundary

Organic synthesis pathways, kinetics, stereochemistry, transition-state chemistry, solvent effects, and condition-dependent competing products require external databases or trained models.

How the chemistry engine works

1. Molecular graph

Every atom is a node. Covalent, aromatic, and ionic interactions are edges. Known molecules are identified by graph isomorphism, so an incorrect H–H–O structure is not recognized as water even though it contains H₂O.

2. Valence and charge

Before a bond is accepted, the engine tests the selected bond order against supported valence states at the atom’s formal charge. Ionic interactions require opposite charges; common charges can be assigned automatically for a typical metal–nonmetal pair.

3. Reactions

The predictor first checks exact textbook reactions, then deterministic families such as complete combustion, neutralization, activity-series displacement, and precipitation. It balances generated products using conservation of elements and charge.

4. Scientific boundary

A universal reaction predictor cannot be derived from reactant identity alone. Real outcomes can depend on solvent, temperature, pressure, catalyst, concentration, phase, light, electrochemical potential, and reaction history. Unsupported cases therefore return “not uniquely predictable” instead of a fabricated equation.

Safety

This application teaches representations and reaction logic. It does not provide experimental quantities, apparatus instructions, or operational synthesis procedures.
